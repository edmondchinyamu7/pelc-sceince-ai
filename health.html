1. How the Human Body Works

Heart and Circulation

The heart pumps blood around the body. Blood transports oxygen, nutrients and other substances.

Respiratory System

The lungs exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide between the air and the blood.

Digestive System

Digestion breaks food down so nutrients can be absorbed and used by the body.

Nervous System

The brain, spinal cord and nerves coordinate movement, sensation, thought and many body functions.

Immune System

The immune system helps recognise and defend the body against harmful pathogens.

Kidneys

The kidneys filter blood, remove waste into urine and help regulate water and salts.

2. Nutrition and Healthy Habits

  • Carbohydrates: an important energy source.
  • Proteins: help build and repair body tissues.
  • Fats: provide energy and help the body absorb certain vitamins.
  • Vitamins and minerals: support normal body functions.
  • Water: supports temperature regulation and many body processes.
  • Fibre: supports healthy digestion.
Healthy needs vary from person to person. Aim for varied meals, safe drinking water, sufficient sleep and activity that suits your age and abilities.

3. Hygiene and Disease Prevention

Wash Hands

Use soap and clean running water, especially before preparing or eating food and after using the toilet.

Safe Water

Use water from a safe source. Follow local public-health guidance on treatment and storage.

Vaccination

Vaccines help the immune system build protection against specific infections. Follow local health-service guidance.

5. First Aid: Know What to Do

When someone may be seriously ill or injured

  1. Check that the area is safe for you and the person.
  2. Get help from a trusted adult or trained first aider.
  3. Contact local emergency services when there is an urgent threat to life.
  4. Follow instructions from emergency dispatchers or qualified health professionals.
  5. Do not give food, drink or medicine to someone who is unconscious.
For severe breathing difficulty, unconsciousness, heavy bleeding or another life-threatening emergency, seek emergency assistance immediately. This page is educational and does not replace professional medical care.
