1. Cell Structure

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living organisms. Animal and plant cells contain a cell membrane, cytoplasm and genetic material. Most plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall, a large central vacuole and chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues.

Simple cell illustration

Nucleus
Cell membrane • Cytoplasm

Simplified illustration for learning; it is not to scale and does not show every cell structure.

Remember:

The nucleus contains most of a eukaryotic cell's DNA. The cell membrane regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell. Mitochondria are important sites of aerobic respiration.

2. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in organic molecules. In plants, it takes place in chloroplasts.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light energy and suitable conditions are required. The equation is a simplified summary of the overall process.

Worked example

If a plant has access to light, water and carbon dioxide, it can make glucose through photosynthesis. The rate is also affected by factors such as light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature.

3. Cellular Respiration

Aerobic respiration releases usable energy from glucose using oxygen. It produces carbon dioxide and water, and transfers energy into ATP.

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy
Key idea:

Photosynthesis stores energy in organic molecules; cellular respiration transfers energy from molecules such as glucose into ATP for cellular work.

4. Ecology and Food Chains

Ecology studies interactions between organisms and their environments. Producers make organic food, consumers obtain energy by feeding, and decomposers break down dead material and recycle nutrients.

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake

Arrows show the direction of energy transfer from food to consumer. Real ecosystems usually contain many interconnected food chains.
