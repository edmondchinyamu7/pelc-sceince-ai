Chemistry Topics

Build your understanding from the basic structure of matter to chemical reactions.

⚛️ Atoms and Elements

An atom contains protons and neutrons in its nucleus, with electrons occupying surrounding energy levels.

Atomic number = Number of protons

🧪 Periodic Table

Elements are arranged by atomic number. Elements in the same group often have similar chemical properties.

Mass number = Protons + Neutrons

🔗 Chemical Bonding

Ionic bonding involves electron transfer; covalent bonding involves shared pairs of electrons.

Example: H₂O, NaCl

⚗️ Chemical Reactions

Reactants change into products. Chemical equations must conserve each type of atom.

Reactants → Products

🧴 Acids and Bases

In aqueous solution, acids increase hydrogen-ion concentration. Bases can accept hydrogen ions.

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]

💨 States of Matter

Solids, liquids and gases differ in particle arrangement, movement and interactions.

Solid ⇄ Liquid ⇄ Gas

🧮 The Mole

The mole is a unit for amount of substance. One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ specified particles.

n = m ÷ M

🌡️ Reaction Rates

Temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts can affect reaction rate.

Rate ≈ Amount changed ÷ Time

🔋 Chemical Energy

Exothermic reactions release energy to the surroundings; endothermic reactions absorb energy.

Energy change: ΔH

1. Atomic Structure

An element is defined by its number of protons. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.

Worked example

A neutral sodium-23 atom has atomic number 11. Protons = 11; electrons = 11; neutrons = 23 − 11 = 12.

2. Chemical Equations

A balanced chemical equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides. Change coefficients in front of formulas to balance an equation; do not change the subscripts inside chemical formulas.

Worked example: formation of water

Unbalanced: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

Balanced: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Both sides contain four hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms.

3. Acids, Bases and pH

At about 25°C, a pH below 7 is acidic, pH 7 is neutral, and a pH above 7 is basic (alkaline). The pH scale is logarithmic, so a one-unit difference corresponds to a tenfold difference in hydrogen-ion concentration.

Worked example

A solution with pH 3 has a hydrogen-ion concentration of approximately 10⁻³ mol/L, assuming the usual dilute aqueous-solution approximation.

Chemistry Formula Library

Amount of Substance

n = m/M

n = amount in mol; m = mass in g; M = molar mass in g/mol.

Concentration

c = n/V

For mol/L, use n in mol and solution volume V in litres.

Particle Number

N = nNₐ

Nₐ ≈ 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

Mass Concentration

Concentration = Mass ÷ Volume

For example, g/L when mass is in grams and volume is in litres.

Density

ρ = m/V

Mass divided by volume.

Percentage Yield

Yield % = Actual ÷ Theoretical × 100

Compare the amount obtained with the theoretical amount.
